\begin{frame}{Metrics do not necessarily agree}
\begin{columns}[T,onlytextwidth]

\begin{column}{.30\linewidth}
\footnotesize
\textbf{How to read}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Each cell compares two metric outputs.
  \item Strong agreement means similar rankings, not identical meaning.
  \item Weak or negative agreement flags a trade-off or protocol effect.
\end{itemize}
\vspace{.2em}
\scriptsize This is benchmark-specific evidence, not a universal ordering.
\end{column}
\end{columns}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Discussion}
\begin{itemize}
  \item A method can score well on faithfulness and poorly on stability.
  \item Metric disagreement is evidence of a trade-off, not a universal winner.
  \item If deletion is out of distribution, what did the score actually test?
  \item What control should a benchmark be able to reject?
\end{itemize}
Answer by naming the property, metric, method, and intended use.
\end{frame}
